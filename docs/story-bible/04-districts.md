# GIHA: District Profiles

## 5.1 Foundry District

### Identity

**Name:** Foundry (named for the old steel mills)

**Population:** 100,000

**Character:** Industrial, working-class, proud, resistant to change

**Visual identity:**
- Brick buildings, smokestacks (now decorative), railroad tracks
- Colors: Rust orange (#E67E22), steel gray (#7F8C8D), brick red (#C0392B)
- Mood: Gritty, hardworking, fading grandeur

### Demographics

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Median age** | 48 |
| **Median income** | $38,000 |
| **Education** | 60% high school or less |
| **Digital access** | 45% mobile-only |
| **Internet trust** | Low |

### Cultural Character

- "We built this city with our hands"
- Deep suspicion of "tech people" who "stole our future"
- Strong community bonds, but insular
- High trust in local figures, low trust in outsiders
- Traditional media consumption (TV, radio, word-of-mouth)

### Vulnerability Profile

**Primary vulnerability:** Economic anxiety + distrust of elites

| Exploitation Type | How It Works |
|-------------------|--------------|
| **Blame narratives** | "The mayor sold us out to tech companies" |
| **Populist rage** | Deepfake of mayor triggers decades of resentment |
| **Nostalgia manipulation** | "Remember when Veritas was great? It's their fault it's not" |
| **Institutional distrust** | "The government doesn't care about people like us" |

### Case 1 Connection (The Viral Mayor)

Foundry is **ground zero** for the mayor deepfake backlash:

- Legacy residents already distrust the mayor
- Deepfake confirms their worst suspicions
- Rage spreads fastest here, then contaminates other districts
- **Intervention target:** Fact-Check Bureau + Community Dialog

### District Statistics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Base σ** | 72 |
| **Base R₀** | 0.8 (higher due to distrust) |
| **Vulnerability** | 0.7 |
| **Intervention cost modifier** | 1.2 (harder to reach, more skepticism) |

## 5.2 Harborview District

### Identity

**Name:** Harborview (waterfront neighborhood)

**Population:** 130,000

**Character:** Mixed residential, waterfront charm, aging population, socially isolated

**Visual identity:**
- Row houses, waterfront promenades, small shops, churches
- Colors: Faded blue (#3498DB), sea green (#1ABC9C), weathered white (#ECF0F1)
- Mood: Quiet, fading beauty, loneliness

### Demographics

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Median age** | 58 |
| **Median income** | $42,000 |
| **Education** | 50% some college or less |
| **Digital access** | 35% mobile-only, 15% no regular access |
| **Internet trust** | Low, but trusts authority voices |

### Cultural Character

- "We're still here, even if the city forgot us"
- Strong church communities, neighborhood associations
- High social isolation (widows, widowers, empty nesters)
- Trust authority figures (doctors, officials, family voices)
- Low digital literacy, high phone dependency

### Vulnerability Profile

**Primary vulnerability:** Social isolation + trust in authority voices

| Exploitation Type | How It Works |
|-------------------|--------------|
| **AI voice scams** | Cloned grandchild voice: "I'm in trouble, I need money" |
| **Authority impersonation** | Fake doctor/hospital calls |
| **Loneliness exploitation** | "Your family is worried about you" |
| **Urgency tactics** | "Act now or your grandchild goes to jail" |

### Case 2 Connection (Grandma's Distress Call)

Harborview is the **primary target** for the voice scam:

- Elderly residents live alone, isolated
- They trust family voices implicitly
- Low technical knowledge = can't detect voice cloning
- High emotional vulnerability = easy to panic
- **Intervention target:** Source Verification Campaign + Community Dialog

### District Statistics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Base σ** | 75 |
| **Base R₀** | 0.5 (low spread, but high victimhood) |
| **Vulnerability** | 0.8 |
| **Intervention cost modifier** | 0.9 (responsive to community programs) |

## 5.3 Uptown District

### Identity

**Name:** Uptown (financial district)

**Population:** 120,000

**Character:** Financial, status-conscious, social media driven, image-obsessed

**Visual identity:**
- Glass towers, upscale shops, coworking spaces, trendy cafes
- Colors: Gold (#F1C40F), charcoal (#2C3E50), white (#ECF0F1)
- Mood: Polished, competitive, anxious beneath the surface

### Demographics

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Median age** | 32 |
| **Median income** | $85,000 |
| **Education** | 70% bachelor's degree or higher |
| **Digital access** | 95% high-speed broadband |
| **Internet trust** | High (but overconfident) |

### Cultural Character

- "Image is everything"
- Heavy social media presence (LinkedIn, Instagram, Twitter)
- Status-conscious, competitive, concerned with reputation
- Believe they're too smart for disinfo (overconfidence = vulnerability)
- Trust "professional" looking content

### Vulnerability Profile

**Primary vulnerability:** Social media driven + image-conscious

| Exploitation Type | How It Works |
|-------------------|--------------|
| **Viral outrage** | Protest photo triggers status anxiety: "Is this who we are?" |
| **Reputation fear** | "If this gets out, Veritas looks bad" |
| **Social proof** | "Everyone is sharing this, it must be true" |
| **Professional credibility** | Fake "expert analysis" spreads because it looks polished |

### Case 3 Connection (The Front Page)

Uptown is the **amplification engine** for the protest photo:

- Status-conscious residents share "important" content to signal virtue
- High social media usage = rapid viral spread
- Overconfidence means they don't verify before sharing
- **Intervention target:** Algorithm Audit + Emergency Broadcast

### District Statistics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Base σ** | 82 |
| **Base R₀** | 0.4 (low baseline, but spikes hard during crisis) |
| **Vulnerability** | 0.6 |
| **Intervention cost modifier** | 0.8 (responsive to professional campaigns) |

## 5.4 Campus District

### Identity

**Name:** Campus (university area)

**Population:** 130,000

**Character:** Academic, idealistic, activist-minded, information-rich but naive

**Visual identity:**
- University buildings, libraries, student housing, protest murals
- Colors: Purple (#9B59B6), green (#2ECC71), chalk white (#F5F5F5)
- Mood: Energetic, idealistic, volatile

### Demographics

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Median age** | 24 |
| **Median income** | $28,000 (students) / $65,000 (faculty/staff) |
| **Education** | 85% bachelor's degree or higher (in progress or completed) |
| **Digital access** | 98% high-speed broadband |
| **Internet trust** | High, but more critical than Uptown |

### Cultural Character

- "Question everything" (but sometimes questions the wrong things)
- High information literacy in theory, emotional in practice
- Strong activist networks, quick to mobilize
- Trust peer validation and independent media
- Vulnerable to narratives that confirm worldview

### Vulnerability Profile

**Primary vulnerability:** Idealism + confirmation bias

| Exploitation Type | How It Works |
|-------------------|--------------|
| **Activist manipulation** | "Share this to fight injustice" |
| **Confirmation bias** | Fake evidence that confirms existing beliefs |
| **Moral urgency** | "If you don't share this, you're complicit" |
| **Peer pressure** | "Everyone in the movement knows this is true" |

### Cross-Cutting Role

Campus doesn't have a dedicated case — it **amplifies all three cases**:

- Students share the mayor deepfake (Case 1) to fuel outrage
- Students share voice scam warnings (Case 2) to "protect the vulnerable"
- Students share protest photo (Case 3) to fuel activism

**Educational irony:** The most "educated" district is most susceptible to emotional manipulation.

### District Statistics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Base σ** | 80 |
| **Base R₀** | 0.7 (high spread due to network effects) |
| **Vulnerability** | 0.65 |
| **Intervention cost modifier** | 0.7 (responsive to academic campaigns) |

## 5.5 District Interaction Dynamics

### How Districts Affect Each Other

| Source District | Target District | Transmission Vector |
|-----------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Foundry → All | Rage narrative | Traditional media, word-of-mouth |
| Harborview → All | Victim narratives | Emotional sharing, concern |
| Uptown → All | Status/viral content | Social media amplification |
| Campus → Uptown | Activist framing | Cross-platform sharing |
| Campus → Foundry | Institutional critique | Counter-narrative alignment |

### Strategic Implications

- **Protect Harborview first:** Low R₀ but high victimhood = early intervention wins
- **Contain Foundry:** High R₀ when triggered = prevent outbreak escalation
- **Monitor Uptown:** Low baseline but explosive when triggered = watch for spikes
- **Engage Campus:** High spread potential = educational interventions most effective here

---

## 5.6 Design Rationale

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| 4 distinct districts | Maps to 3 cases + cross-cutting amplifier |
| Age/economic diversity | Different vulnerabilities = different disinfo types |
| Foundry as rage center | Economic anxiety = most explosive when triggered |
| Harborview as victim center | Isolation = most emotionally vulnerable |
| Uptown as amplifier | Status-conscious = fastest viral spread |
| Campus as wildcard | Educated but naive = teaches "literacy ≠ immunity" |
