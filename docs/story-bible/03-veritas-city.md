# GIHA: Veritas City Profile

## 4.1 City Identity

**Name:** Veritas (Latin for "truth")

**Type:** Post-industrial coastal revival city

**Population:** 500,000

**Location:** Fictional coastal city, inspired by post-industrial American/European cities

**Geography:** Harbor city with river dividing north/south, industrial waterfront transitioning to tech campuses

**Climate:** Temperate, foggy mornings, mild winters

### City Motto

> *"Veritas Vos Liberabit"* — The Truth Will Set You Free

**Ironic twist:** A city named "truth" drowning in disinfo

## 4.2 Economic Profile

### Historical Context

| Era | Economy | Legacy |
|-----|---------|--------|
| **1950-1980** | Manufacturing boom | Shipbuilding, steel, textiles — middle-class prosperity |
| **1980-2000** | Deindustrialization | Factory closures, job losses, population decline |
| **2000-2020** | Tech revival | Startups, remote workers, "innovation hub" branding |
| **2020-present** | Uneasy coexistence | Old-timers vs. newcomers, economic anxiety |

### Current Economy

| Sector | % of Jobs | Character |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **Technology** | 28% | Startups, remote workers, high-paying |
| **Healthcare** | 22% | Hospitals, clinics, stable employment |
| **Education** | 15% | University, community colleges |
| **Service** | 20% | Restaurants, retail, hospitality |
| **Legacy manufacturing** | 10% | Shrinking, automation threats |
| **Other** | 5% | Government, non-profit |

### Economic Tension (MIL Vulnerability)

The city is split between:

- **New arrivals:** Tech workers, young professionals, optimistic about digital future
- **Legacy residents:** Former factory workers, older, distrustful of change, feel left behind
- **The gap:** Economic inequality, cultural resentment, competing narratives about who "deserves" Veritas

**Why this matters for disinfo:**
- Economic anxiety makes people susceptible to narratives that blame "the other side"
- Legacy residents distrust institutions that "abandoned" them
- New arrivals are overconfident in their digital literacy
- Both groups are vulnerable — just to different types of disinfo

### Information Vulnerabilities

| Vulnerability | Description | Exploitation |
|---------------|-------------|--------------|
| **Platform silos** | Different demographics use different platforms | Disinfo can target each silo separately |
| **Trust asymmetry** | Legacy residents trust traditional media; new arrivals trust social | Different credibility signals for different groups |
| **Local pride** | "Veritas is special" narrative | Makes outsiders' warnings seem like attacks |
| **Economic grievance** | Deep resentment about factory closures | Blame narratives find fertile ground |

## 4.6 Why Veritas Is Targeted

The Shadow Collective chose Veritas for specific reasons:

1. **Perfect vulnerability mix:** Economic anxiety + tech hub = disinfo breeding ground
2. **Media fragmentation:** Easy to target different demographics separately
3. **Institutional weakness:** City government is underfunded, slow to respond
4. **National attention:** Tech revival story makes Veritas "interesting" — attacks here get amplified
5. **Symbolic value:** City named "truth" falling to lies is narratively powerful

## 4.7 City as Character

Veritas isn't just a backdrop — it's a **character** that the player must protect.

**Personality:** Proud, anxious, divided, hopeful

**Arc:**
- **Start:** Tense but stable (σ = 78, R₀ = 0.6)
- **Middle:** Under attack,裂缝s showing (outbreaks, rising R₀)
- **End:** Depends on player — resilient or trapped

**Visual representation:**
- **Healthy:** Warm colors, busy streets, lights on
- **Infected:** Desaturated colors, empty streets, flickering lights
- **σ-trap:** Gray palette, abandoned buildings, fog

## 4.8 Design Rationale

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Post-industrial setting | Economic anxiety = natural MIL vulnerability |
| City named "truth" | Ironic, memorable, thematically resonant |
| 4 districts | Maps to 3 cases + cross-cutting campus |
| Age/economic split | Different vulnerabilities for different disinfo types |
| Media fragmentation | Teaches: platform matters for information behavior |

## 4.9 Implementation Notes

- City visual: Top-down isometric view with 4 district quadrants
- Health indicators: Color-coded heatmap overlay
- District borders: Visible but not rigid (disinfo crosses boundaries)
- Population counters: Real-time S/E/I/R display
- Intervention effects: Visible in city aesthetics (lights turning on, people returning)
