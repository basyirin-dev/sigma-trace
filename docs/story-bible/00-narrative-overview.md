# GIHA: Narrative Overview

## 1.1 Story Hook

**Opening narration (displayed on game start):**

> *Three months ago, you joined GIHA — the Global Information Health Agency.*
>
> *Your first case was a deepfake that destroyed a journalist's career. The video showed them confessing to crimes they never committed. By the time you traced the fabrication, their reputation was ash. You saved them — barely — but the damage was done.*
>
> *Now, a new threat: Veritas. A mid-sized city on the coast, caught between its industrial past and digital future. Someone is tearing it apart with lies.*
>
> *The mayor is under attack. The elderly are being scammed. The streets are burning with fabricated outrage.*
>
> *This is your case. This is your chance to make it right.*

## 1.2 Core Themes

| Theme | How It Appears |
|-------|----------------|
| **Trust is fragile** | A single deepfake can destroy a career; a single scam can devastate a family |
| **Vulnerability is contextual** | Economic anxiety, age, status — different pressures create different weaknesses |
| **Truth requires work** | Authenticity isn't self-evident; investigation is a skill, not a gut feeling |
| **Systems have cracks** | Insider threats, institutional gaps, human weakness — exploitation is always possible |
| **Follow the money** | Ideology often masks financial motives; question who benefits |

## 1.3 Narrative Tone

**Genre:** Investigative thriller with educational grounding

**Influences:**
- *Spotlight* (methodical investigation, institutional accountability)
- *Mr. Robot* (digital forensics, systems thinking)
- *The Social Dilemma* (real-world MIL relevance)

**Emotional arc:**
1. **Act I (Case 1):** Discovery — player learns the threat is real and coordinated
2. **Act II (Case 2):** Escalation — personal stakes, vulnerable victims, moral complexity
3. **Act III (Case 3):** Climax — city-wide crisis, all threads converge

**Tone shifts by mode:**
- **Strategy mode:** Analytical, systemic, bird's-eye view
- **Detective mode:** Intimate, focused, ground-level investigation
- **Transitions:** Brief cutscenes bridge the macro/micro perspectives

## 1.4 Educational Integration

Every narrative element teaches Media and Information Literacy:

| Story Element | MIL Lesson |
|---------------|------------|
| Mayor deepfake (Case 1) | Deepfakes exploit trust in authority; multi-modal verification is essential |
| Voice scam (Case 2) | AI voice cloning is accessible; verify through independent channels |
| Protest photo (Case 3) | Reverse image search and geolocation are fundamental verification skills |
| District vulnerabilities | Disinfo campaigns are *targeted*, not random; context determines susceptibility |
| Mira (insider) | Economic vulnerability enables exploitation; systemic solutions needed |
| Shadow Collective | Financial motives often hide behind ideological rhetoric |

## 1.5 Pacing

| Phase | Duration | Narrative Beat |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| **Intro** | 60s | Opening narration, GIHA briefing, deploy to Veritas |
| **Case 1** | 3-5 min | First deepfake, player learns investigation mechanics |
| **Strategy interlude** | 1-2 min | City health check, intervention deployment |
| **Case 2** | 3-5 min | Voice scam, discover Mira, moral complexity |
| **Strategy interlude** | 1-2 min | Escalating crisis, higher stakes |
| **Case 3** | 3-5 min | Protest photo, all evidence converges |
| **Finale** | 2-3 min | Final verdict, city outcome, debrief |

**Total playtime:** 15-20 minutes (one session)

## 1.6 Victory and Failure Narratives

### Victory (σ ≥ 80, R₀ < 0.8)
> *Veritas is stable. The Shadow Collective's network has been exposed. The city will recover — not because disinfo was eliminated, but because its citizens learned to question, verify, and think critically.*
>
> *You didn't just solve a case. You built resilience.*

### Failure (σ < 20)
> *Veritas is trapped. The σ-coherence has collapsed. Citizens retreat into echo chambers, trusting only narratives that confirm their fears.*
>
> *The Collective got what they wanted: a city divided against itself.*
>
> *But this isn't the end. Every failure teaches. Next time, you'll see the patterns sooner.*

## 1.7 Design Principles

1. **Story serves education** — Every narrative choice must reinforce MIL concepts
2. **Complexity without confusion** — Moral ambiguity adds depth, not paralysis
3. **Respect the player** — No heavy-handed lecturing; let evidence speak
4. **Systemic thinking** — Individual cases connect to larger patterns
5. **Hope through competence** — Victory is possible through skill, not luck
