# GIHA: 3-Minute Pitch Script

**Format:** Screen recording with voiceover
**Duration:** 180 seconds (±5)
**Tone:** Urgent, optimistic, grounded in real problem → real solution

---

## TIMECODE KEY
`[MM:SS]` — timestamp
`VO:` — voiceover line
`SCREEN:` — what the viewer sees
`SFX:` — sound effect

---

### [00:00–00:15] Hook

`SFX:` Phone buzz notification. Cut to black.

`VO:` Someone in your city just received this voice note. A frightened grandmother. She thinks her grandson is in jail. She's about to send money.

`SCREEN:` Dark screen. Text fades in: *"Grandma, it's me — I need bail money, please don't tell Mom."*

`VO:` The voice is AI-generated. The number is spoofed. The love is real. And in 2026, she has no way to know the difference.

`SFX:` A soft, unsettling glitch in the audio. Pause.

---

### [00:15–00:45] The Problem

`VO:` Disinformation isn't just fake news anymore. It's your mayor's face saying things he never said. It's your grandparent's voice cloned by a scammer. It's a photograph of a protest that never happened.

`SCREEN:` Rapid cuts — a deepfake video of a politician, an audio wave visualization, a photo with red X marks over lighting inconsistencies. Each flashes for 2 seconds. End on a world map showing hotspots.

`VO:` Traditional media literacy teaches you to fact-check. But fact-checking is too slow. By the time the truth arrives, the damage is done.

`VO:` The problem isn't that people are stupid. The problem is that the ecosystem is designed for maximum viral spread — not maximum coherence.

`SCREEN:` The R₀ indicator crosses 1.0 — the critical threshold for epidemic spread. The sigma gauge begins to trend downward.

---

### [00:45–01:30] The Solution

`VO:` We built GIHA. A game that teaches you to think like an AI forensics investigator and act like an information ecosystem manager.

`SCREEN:` Title card: **GIHA** with the sigma symbol glowing.

`VO:` Two modes, one mission.

`SCREEN:` Split screen appears:
- Left half: Strategy mode — city grid with colored heatmaps
- Right half: Detective mode — evidence board with forensic tools

`VO:` In Strategy Mode, you manage a city's information health. Monitor the R₀ — the spread rate of disinformation. Deploy interventions. Watch the σ-coherence rise or fall.

`SCREEN:` Focus on left half. Show the city grid. Click an intervention card. The heatmap overlay cools from red to green as the intervention takes effect.

`VO:` When a case breaks — a deepfake, a voice scam, a manipulated photo — you zoom in to Detective Mode.

`SCREEN:` Transition animation — screen whooshes, left half expands to full view. Now showing the evidence board.

`VO:` Here, you're the forensics investigator. Use a spectrogram to detect synthetic audio artifacts. Step through a video frame by frame to find the splice. Reverse-image-search a photo to catch the lie.

`SCREEN:` Quick demo of each tool. A spectrogram reveals missing frequencies in the voice clip. Frame-stepping shows a jump in the video. Source tracer shows the photo first appeared in a different country.

`VO:` Every tool you use teaches you a real MIL skill. Every case you solve improves your city's health. Every failure — and you will fail — shows you exactly what happens to a population without media literacy.

`SCREEN:` Brief flash of game-over screen: "The city has entered a σ-trap. Coherence collapsed."

---

### [01:30–02:15] The Math Behind It

`VO:` Under the hood, GIHA runs on something no other MIL tool has: a mathematical model of belief coherence.

`SCREEN:` Simple diagram. Equations dissolve into visual graphs.

`VO:` The σ-coherence metric — adapted from our research on neural network training dynamics — models how populations process information. The R₀ threshold tells you when disinformation has gone from containable to epidemic.

`SCREEN:` An animation of the phase transition. A graph line is green, then crosses a red threshold (R₀ = 1.0), and the screen desaturates. Text: "Transcritical Bifurcation."

`VO:` We're not teaching MIL through lectures or quizzes. We're putting you inside the system. When you watch a city collapse because you ignored the early warning signs, you internalize the lesson. Not because we told you to. Because you felt it.

---

### [02:15–02:50] Why This Matters

`SCREEN:` Cut to photos of real communities — a market in West Africa, a classroom in Brazil, a village in India.

`VO:` Media literacy isn't a luxury for educated Western youth. It's a survival skill for everyone. In communities where disinformation has sparked violence, where scam calls drain life savings, where manipulated news topples governments — the people who need MIL the most have the least access to it.

`VO:` GIHA is a web app. No install. No server. No internet required after load. It runs on a $150 Chromebook. It supports localization. And it costs zero dollars to deploy.

`SCREEN:` The URL: sigmatrace.io — then transition to the game running in a browser.

`VO:` We are two developers. One AI researcher who built the σ-coherence formalism. One cybersecurity engineer who builds trust architectures. We combined our skills to build something that didn't exist before: a game that makes the invisible structure of disinformation visible.

---

### [02:50–03:00] Call to Action

`SCREEN:` GIHA logo. URL. Text: "Play Your Part."

`VO:` Disinformation leaves a trace. GIHA teaches you to follow it. Play your part.

`SFX:` Case solved chime.

`SCREEN:` Fade to black. UNESCO Youth Hackathon 2026 logo.
